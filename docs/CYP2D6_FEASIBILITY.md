# CYP2D6 Feasibility Writeup

CYP2D6 has been listed as explicitly out of scope since Phase 0 (see `README.md`'s "Explicitly out of scope" line and the project plan §9), with a one-line reason: it needs specialist structural-variant-aware calling this project deliberately isn't attempting. This document works that reason through in real technical detail, against real primary sources, so "out of scope" is a traceable engineering conclusion rather than an assertion. It is a research writeup, not an implementation plan — nothing here is a commitment to build CYP2D6 support, and nothing here is a criticism of the specialist tools discussed; several of them are exactly the right answer to a problem this project's architecture was never built to solve.

## 1. Why CYP2D6 is a genuinely different problem than every gene this project already implements

Every gene module in this project (`genes/tpmt.py`, `genes/dpyd.py`, `genes/slco1b1.py`, `genes/cyp2c19.py`, `genes/nudt15.py`) shares one architectural assumption, inherited directly from `normalize.py`'s `parse_vcf()`: a small, fixed set of defining chromosomal positions is read out of an input VCF as ordinary SNVs/indels, and star alleles are assigned by pattern-matching the genotypes found there. This works because TPMT, DPYD, SLCO1B1, CYP2C19, and NUDT15's clinically important alleles are, overwhelmingly, defined by SNVs and small indels at a handful of positions.

CYP2D6 breaks that assumption at the locus level, not just at the allele-catalog level. It sits at chromosome 22q13.2 immediately next to two non-functional paralogous pseudogenes, *CYP2D7* and *CYP2D8*, all three sharing nine exons and a high degree of sequence identity (Kimura S et al. 1989, *Am J Hum Genet* 45:889-904, the gene's original characterization). PharmVar's own structural variation reference document for CYP2D6 (`Variation_CYP2D6.pdf`, v1.6, 2019-11-06, fetched directly from pharmvar.org) opens by stating the locus "contains three genes, CYP2D6, CYP2D7 and CYP2D8... All three genes are composed of nine exons and share a high degree of sequence similarity," with CYP2D6 and CYP2D7 additionally sharing "a common, 0.6 kb long downstream region" and "near-identical repetitive sequences referred to as REP6 and REP7." A 1.6 kb "spacer" sequence between that shared region and REP7 is the one structural feature that reliably distinguishes CYP2D7's downstream region from CYP2D6's — and, as detailed in §2 below, whether a given CYP2D6 gene copy ends in REP6, REP7, REPdel, or REPdup is itself diagnostic of which star allele it is.

Two consequences follow directly, and both are upstream of anything a gene module in this project's current architecture could fix:

- **Short-read alignment itself becomes unreliable near CYP2D6.** Reads originating from CYP2D6 can misalign to CYP2D7 coordinates and vice versa, because the sequences are close to identical over long stretches. A VCF produced by a standard short-read variant-calling pipeline can therefore already contain spurious or missing genotype calls at CYP2D6's defining positions before this project's `parse_vcf()` ever reads it — this is not a bug this project's normalization logic could catch or correct, because by the time a VCF exists, the alignment-level information needed to tell "this read is really CYP2D6" from "this read is really CYP2D7, misaligned" is already gone.
- **A large share of CYP2D6's clinically important alleles are not SNV/indel-defined at all — they are copy-number and structural-rearrangement events.** No VCF genotype at a fixed position can represent "this sample has three copies of the gene" or "this gene copy is a CYP2D7→CYP2D6 hybrid." That information lives in read depth and structural/breakpoint signal across the whole locus, not in per-position genotypes.

## 2. The real scope of CYP2D6 structural variation (PharmVar's own catalog)

PharmVar currently defines CYP2D6*1 through *172 (163 distinct star alleles, 9 star numbers retired) — already roughly 30x the allele count any single gene module in this project implements. The majority are ordinary SNV/indel-defined alleles, the kind this project's architecture already handles well for other genes. The structural subset, however, is extensive and directly documented by PharmVar (`Variation_CYP2D6.pdf`, quoted directly below):

- **Whole-gene deletion (`*5`).** "The allelic variant defined as CYP2D6*5 is characterized by a deletion of the entire CYP2D6 gene... the REP region for this allele is referred to as REPdel." PharmVar notes it "remains unknown... whether all deletion alleles identified as CYP2D6*5 have identical breakpoints."
- **Duplications and multiplications.** Reported for normal-function alleles (`*1`, `*2`), decreased-function alleles (`*41`), and even nonfunctional alleles (`*4`) — copy number alone does not imply function, and a nonfunctional allele duplicated is still nonfunctional. PharmVar's own duplication/multiplication table (Table 2 in the source document) lists real literature-reported cases for `*1x2/xN`, `*2x2/xN`, `*4x2/xN`, `*6x2`, `*10x2/xN`, `*17x2`, `*29x2`, `*35x2`, `*41x2/xN`, `*43x2`, and `*45x2`, each with its own PMID trail. PharmVar states plainly: **"there are currently no standards describing how to textually display CYP2D6 CNVs"** — diplotype notation itself (`*1/*5` vs `(*1/*1) x1` vs `(*1/*5) 1N`) is inconsistent across labs, a real, unresolved reporting problem this project's `PGxResult` schema (one diplotype string per gene) was never designed to represent.
- **CYP2D6→CYP2D7 hybrid genes (5′ CYP2D6, 3′ CYP2D7)**, including `*10.003`, `*61`, `*63`, `*68`, and `*4.013` — these typically carry a REPdel-like downstream region and, per PharmVar, "may support amplification with certain XL-PCR-based CYP2D6*5 assays leading to false-positive CYP2D6*5 calls," i.e. even orthogonal wet-lab CNV assays can be fooled by this locus's structure.
- **CYP2D7→CYP2D6 hybrid genes (5′ CYP2D7, 3′ CYP2D6)**, consolidated under a single designation, `CYP2D6*13`, marked by a hallmark T-insertion in exon 1 that renders them nonfunctional.
- **Tandem arrangements** — two or more non-identical gene copies on one chromosome, at least one usually a hybrid — including `*36+*10` (the most common East Asian tandem, decreased function), `*13+*68+*4`, `*13+*1x2`, `*1x2+*83`, and `*4.013+*4`. PharmVar's document adds that "expert panel members involved with clinical testing have observed test results that suggest the existence of numerous additional CNV structures... that have not been further characterized, published or submitted to PharmVar" — the true structural catalog is understood to be incomplete even by the consortium that maintains it.

None of `ObservedVariant`, `PGxResult`, or any gene module in this project has a representation for "a REP6→REP7 junction," "gene copy number," or "hybrid breakpoint location." Adding CYP2D6 would not be a new gene module written in the existing style; it would require new data model concepts this project's schema (Phase 1, `models.py`/`schema.py`) does not have.

## 3. Even the specialist tools built for exactly this problem don't agree with each other

This project's existing gene modules are each validated to be internally consistent against hand-derived, cited expected outcomes (Plan §3a's "never silently guess" discipline) and, where possible, real GeT-RM reference material. CYP2D6 is unusual among pharmacogenes in that the field itself has already built and published multiple specialist bioinformatics tools for exactly this calling problem, and a systematic head-to-head comparison of them exists — which makes it possible to ask, directly, "how hard is this, really?" using real published numbers rather than a general impression.

Nassar (npj Genomic Medicine 2021, DOI `10.1038/s41525-020-0135-2`, "A systematic comparison of pharmacogene star allele calling bioinformatics algorithms: a focus on CYP2D6 genotyping") compared three purpose-built CYP2D6 callers — Astrolabe (formerly Constellation), Aldy, and Stargazer — on 4,618 simulated diplotypes plus 75 real whole-genome-sequenced GeT-RM samples (the CDC's own reference-material program, the same source this project already cites for TPMT/DPYD/CYP2C19/SLCO1B1/NUDT15 validation). Findings, quoted directly from the paper's own abstract and results:

- On the simplest possible test — homozygous, SNV-only-defined diplotypes, no structural variation at all — the three tools' haplotype concordance against ground truth was **92% (Astrolabe), 96% (Aldy), and 88% (Stargazer)**, out of 154 test cases. Even in the easy case, none of the three specialist tools was fully correct.
- "For all three tools, the concordance for haplotypes was much higher than that for diplotypes. This underscores the challenge of consistently calling both haplotypes for each sample in a highly polymorphic gene such as CYP2D6" — i.e. getting one haplotype right is measurably easier than getting both haplotypes of a real diplotype right, and diplotype accuracy is what a clinical report actually needs.
- Aldy performed best on structural variants, Stargazer second, and "Astrolabe had limitations especially in calling hybrid rearrangements" — the exact `*13`/`*36`/tandem category described in §2 above.
- An ensemble approach (consensus across all three tools) improved haplotype concordance but "is prone to ambiguities whenever complete discrepancies between the tools arise" — running multiple specialist callers and averaging their answers does not eliminate the disagreement, it just relocates it to "which tool do we trust when they disagree."
- The paper's own framing of current wet-lab gold-standard CYP2D6 genotyping: "a battery of methods e.g. quantitative PCR analysis for CNV detection, allele specific XL-PCR, SMRT sequencing, and Sanger sequencing... expensive, laborious and unscalable" — even outside bioinformatics entirely, there is no single simple assay.

Two tools published after that comparison are worth noting directly, since they represent the current state of the art rather than the field as of 2020:

- **Cyrius** (Twist GP et al. 2021, *The Pharmacogenomics Journal*, DOI `10.1038/s41397-020-00205-5`), built at Illumina specifically to improve on the tools above using WGS read-depth and small-variant signal together. Its validation against 138 GeT-RM reference samples plus 8 PacBio HiFi long-read samples (covering 40 known star alleles) found 96.5% concordance with truth genotypes initially, improved to 99.3% after fixes identified during that same validation — a real, current best-in-class result, and still not 100%, on a validation set that (unlike a real unselected clinical population) was deliberately chosen to be enriched for known, catalogued star alleles.
- **PharmCAT**, which this project's own `docs/VALIDATION.md` §4 already documents attempting (and being unable, in this sandbox) to run live against, **does not genotype CYP2D6 itself at all** — per the comparison paper above, it "uses Astrolabe's allele calling output for its unique clinical annotation step." Even the reference clinical annotation tool this project has already benchmarked against for every other gene delegates CYP2D6 star-allele calling to a separate, specialized tool rather than implementing its own.

The honest conclusion from this section: CYP2D6 diplotype calling at genuinely high accuracy is not a solved problem even among tools built by specialist teams specifically to solve it, using full aligned sequencing data this project's VCF-only input never has access to. This is not a gap specific to this project's implementation; it is close to the current edge of what the field has published.

## 4. The phenotype layer would also be new: the CPIC/DPWG Activity Score system

Independent of the calling problem in §§1-3, CYP2D6's phenotype-assignment model needed on top of a correct diplotype is its own real piece of work, defined by Caudle KE et al. 2020 (*Clinical and Translational Science* 13:116-124, DOI `10.1111/cts.12692`, "Standardizing CYP2D6 Genotype to Phenotype Translation: Consensus Recommendations from the Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium and Dutch Pharmacogenetics Working Group"), the current CPIC/DPWG consensus standard (reached via modified-Delphi methodology across 37 international CYP2D6 experts over 7 survey rounds, 82% final agreement).

Each allele is assigned an activity value from 0 to 1 (0 = no function, 0.25/0.5/1.0 = decreased tiers/normal function, >1.0 for select increased-function alleles), and a diplotype's activity score is the sum of its two alleles' values. Phenotype bands, per the consensus paper: Ultrarapid Metabolizer (AS > 2.25), Normal Metabolizer (AS 1.25-2.25), Intermediate Metabolizer (AS 0.25-1), Poor Metabolizer (AS 0). Two details from the 2020 consensus update are worth noting because they show this mapping is still actively being refined, not settled trivia: `CYP2D6*10`'s own activity value was revised downward from 0.5 to 0.25 in this update, and a diplotype with activity score exactly 1 was reclassified from Normal Metabolizer to Intermediate Metabolizer — both real changes to real patients' phenotype calls, from the same guideline body this project already cites for every other gene, underscoring that even the phenotype-assignment side (not just the diplotype-calling side) of CYP2D6 is more actively revised than the diplotype-lookup or single-activity-score models this project's other five genes use.

Architecturally, this activity-score summation model is not entirely novel to this project — `genes/dpyd.py` (Phase 3) already implements an activity-score model for DPYD, so the phenotype-assignment *shape* has precedent (see `docs/ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW_V01.md`). What would be new is the diplotype input feeding it: DPYD's activity score sums over a small number of SNV/indel-defined alleles from a fixed position set; CYP2D6's would need to sum over a diplotype whose calling, per §§1-3, cannot be produced by this project's existing VCF-only, position-list-driven Layer 1 at all.

## 5. What a real v2 implementation would require

Stated plainly, in order of how fundamental the change is:

1. **Alignment-level input, not VCF-only.** Read-depth-based copy number detection (needed for `*5` deletions and the `*1xN`/`*2xN`/`*4xN`/etc. duplication family) and structural/breakpoint detection (needed for hybrid and tandem alleles) both require access to aligned reads (BAM/CRAM) across the full CYP2D6/CYP2D7/CYP2D8 locus, not just genotype calls at a short list of positions. This is a different Layer 1 than every other gene in this project uses, not an extension of `normalize.py`'s existing `parse_vcf()`.
2. **A copy-number layer.** Something that computes CYP2D6 copy number relative to a stable reference region from normalized read depth — entirely absent from this project today.
3. **A structural/breakpoint layer.** Something that discriminates REP6 vs. REP7 vs. REPdel vs. REPdup downstream regions and locates hybrid switch points (the `*13`/`*36`/`*61`/`*63`/`*68`/`*4.013` family), and resolves tandem gene order (which copy is "upstream," per PharmVar's own tandem-naming convention).
4. **A new `ObservedVariant`-equivalent representation** for structural events — this project's current model (`chrom, pos, ref, alt`) has no way to express "gene copy 2 of 3 is a CYP2D7→CYP2D6 hybrid with a switch point in intron 7."
5. **A CYP2D6-specific diplotype assembler** that combines (2)-(4) with ordinary SNV/indel genotyping for the SNV-defined majority of alleles — genuinely more complex than any diplotype-resolution logic this project has built so far (TPMT/NUDT15's no-function-allele counting, SLCO1B1's transport-function lookup, CYP2C19's three-independent-loci compound-diplotype resolution, DPYD's activity-score summation).
6. **The Activity Score phenotype layer** described in §4, on top of a correct diplotype.

Two realistic paths exist for (1)-(5), and they are worth naming explicitly rather than leaving implicit:

- **Wrap an existing specialist tool** (most plausibly Cyrius, given its current best-published concordance and its explicit hg19/hg38 support) as an external caller: shell out to it against a BAM/CRAM input, parse its diplotype output, and adapt that into this project's `PGxResult` shape — architecturally similar to how `cli.py` already shells out to nothing but itself today, but conceptually identical to how PharmCAT itself delegates CYP2D6 to Astrolabe rather than reimplementing star-allele calling (§3). This is the realistic path if CYP2D6 support were ever added: it does not require this project to solve a problem multiple specialist research teams have spent years on, and it is consistent with this project's own stated identity (`README.md`: "not a universal star-allele caller... not a complete CYP2D6 solution").
- **Implement (1)-(6) from scratch.** Explicitly not recommended: per §3, this is close to the current edge of what published, peer-reviewed, team-built tools achieve, and redoing that work from scratch is a multi-year research undertaking, not a scope extension appropriate for this project.

## 6. What this writeup deliberately does not do

This is a feasibility assessment, not an implementation. No code changes accompany it. It does not evaluate Cyrius, Aldy, Stargazer, or Astrolabe's licenses for integration (a real next step if the "wrap a specialist tool" path were ever pursued — see `docs/DATA_SOURCES_AND_LICENSING.md`'s existing licensing-audit discipline for how this project would approach that). It does not attempt to estimate implementation effort in time, since that depends on a build-vs-wrap decision this document only frames, not makes.
